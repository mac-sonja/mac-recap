# PRD Quality Review — MAC Recap – MVP PRD (v2)

Reviewed: `prd.md` (v2, Oct 8, 2026) and `addendum.md`. Stakes: internal initiative, CEO go/no-go demo on Oct 30, 2026. The v1 document structure and stable FR/NFR/epic numbering were kept on purpose, so template-shape deviations are not counted as defects.

## Overall verdict

The v2 update is honest and well scoped. The thesis is clear: assemble verified material, never generate from scratch. The changes from v1 are stated as decisions. Out of scope and known risks admit that the root cause stays open, which is rare and useful. What's at risk is build-readiness for Sprint 2 (due Oct 21), for three reasons. Several FRs and stories have no testable "done". The guardrails conflict with the multi-format export and identity model in ways the PRD doesn't flag, even though its own rule says "must be flagged". And open points 1, 3, 8, 9 and 11 block epics due in 6–13 days, with no owner or decision date.

## Decision-readiness — adequate

The change log (lines 16–25) states decisions as decisions: standalone MVP, Oct 30 as a demo not a pilot, the merged roles, "publish" meaning a shareable link, Epic 1 removed. Trade-offs are named honestly. §11 says the MVP "may not make them happen", and §1.1 line 62 separates layout hours from the 24–60 hour end-to-end baseline. The open points are genuinely open, and points 9 and 11 sit at real tensions.

The weakness is timing. §12 says the open points "must be resolved before the epics that depend on them", but none has an owner or a decision date. Several fall due within days of today (Oct 8): point 3, the taxonomy, before Epic 2 (Sprint 1, Oct 14); points 1, 2 and 8, before Epics 3–4 (Oct 21); points 9 and 11, before Epics 5–6. The PRD also doesn't say what happens on a no-go at the demo.

### Findings
- **high** Blocking open points have no owner or deadline (§12, §6) — Open points 1 (export formats), 3 (taxonomy), 8 (status lifecycle), 9 (owner export rights) and 11 (link access) gate Sprint 1–2 epics, but nobody is accountable for closing them. Open point 1 alone decides whether Epic 3 builds templates for 1 format or 8 (the eight are listed on line 294). *Fix:* add an owner and a "decide by" date to each open point, tied to the sprint it blocks. Mark which ones have to be closed by Oct 14.
- **medium** Demo outcome paths are undefined (§1.1, line 42) — "go / no-go with the CEO" has success criteria, but nothing says what a no-go means: stop, iterate, or delay. Nothing says whether all four criteria must pass. *Fix:* one line on the pass rule (all four, or a named subset) and the consequence of a no-go.

## Substance over theater — strong

There are no personas, only three functional roles, and each role drives permissions (Story 6.2). The NFRs are product-specific guardrails, not "scalable / secure" boilerplate. NFR4's carve-out ("Time spent waiting for the draft to generate does not count") is a real, earned decision. The problem statement in §1 is concrete and uses numbers (4 of 11, ~2 months, 1–2 h layout). The only furniture-like spots are where a requirement points to an artifact that doesn't exist yet.

### Findings
- **medium** NFR5 relies on an undefined "quality checklist" (§4 NFR5) — "the quality checklist" is a Sprint 2 non-dev deliverable ("narrative structure and quality criteria"). That makes NFR5 currently unverifiable, and it is a demo success criterion (line 46). *Fix:* mark NFR5's checklist as a dependency with a due date. Or inline the minimum criteria, for example "names at least one technical decision and one design decision".

## Strategic coherence — strong

The thesis is stated (line 29) and applied consistently: NFR6 (no material, no generation), FR3 "using only the uploaded material", and FR10 deferred because it would generate from order data. Prioritization follows the core flow (upload → generate → review → export), with authentication deliberately last. The success metrics validate the thesis at three horizons, and the counter-metric (line 65) protects the claim check against speed pressure. That is exactly the right counter-metric for this thesis. §11 openly says the MVP doesn't attack the A3 root cause, and the 6-month "share of closed projects" metric measures that gap.

### Findings
- **medium** The guardrail metric rewards not catching hallucinations (§1.1, line 54) — "Hallucinations or guardrail violations caught in review, against a target of zero" rewards a weak review: fewer catches look better. That runs against the counter-metric on line 65. *Fix:* split it into "hallucinations reaching an approved case study: target 0" and "hallucinations caught in review: tracked, no target", which measures generation quality.
- **low** No instrumentation for the 8-week measures (§1.1, lines 49–54) — owner hands-on time, manual edit volume and catches need capture, but there is no FR and no stated manual method. *Fix:* state that measurement is manual (stopwatch or log sheet) or add a lightweight FR, such as timestamps for upload start and approval plus an edit count.

## Done-ness clarity — thin

This is the weakest dimension, and it's the one story creation will lean on. Several FRs and acceptance criteria are tautological or point elsewhere. Some core behaviours are missing entirely: text editing, post-approval edits, and per-user identity for the approval rule.

### Findings
- **high** No requirement lets the owner edit the generated text (§3 FR5, §2.1 "Configurator", Story 4.2) — The configurator is defined as adjusting "layout, media and typography". Story 4.2's only acceptance criterion is "follows the designed Bento layout". Nothing lets the owner/designer correct a hallucinated or wrong claim found during the NFR2 check. Yet "Amount of manual editing needed after generation" (line 53) is a validation measure. Without text editing, the NFR2 review can only reject, not fix. *Fix:* add to FR5 and Story 4.2 that the owner/designer can edit the challenge/approach/result text, and say whether regenerating a section is allowed.
- **high** The approval-ownership rule can't be enforced with the planned identity model (FR6, NFR7, FR8, Story 6.1, line 20) — FR6 and NFR7 say approval "belongs to the owner/designer who uploaded the visual material". But (a) Epic 4 ships in Sprint 2 with no authentication, so there is no uploader identity. (b) Line 20 and Story 6.1 describe logins per role ("gets its own login", "a login for each of the three roles"), which reads as shared role credentials, not per-user accounts. (c) Nothing covers several uploaders, or an owner/designer who is someone other than the uploader. *Fix:* state that login is per user. Add an acceptance criterion to Epic 6 that adds uploader identity to approval. Decide whether "who uploaded" means one person or any owner/designer on the case study.
- **medium** Behaviour after approval is undefined (FR6, Story 4.3) — Can an approved case study still be edited? If it is edited, does it lose Approved status, and do existing shareable links and exports update? The addendum mentions versioning and autosave (addendum line 19), but the PRD doesn't. This directly affects NFR7. *Fix:* add an acceptance criterion: "Editing an approved case study returns it to Awaiting approval; existing links show the last approved version (or are withdrawn)".
- **medium** Weak or tautological acceptance criteria (Stories 2.3, 3.2, 4.1, 4.2, 6.1) — "The file and text upload flow is complete" (2.3); "The adaptation rules defined in the final template are applied" (3.2, which defers to an undelivered artifact); "follows the designed Bento layout" (4.2). Story 4.1 lists only awaiting-review items, though the glossary defines the dashboard as all statuses with search and filter. *Fix:* replace these with observable conditions, such as file-type rejection, the 20 × 100 MB limits from FR2, minimum and maximum media counts per template, and the dashboard filter by status.
- **medium** NFR6 hinges on an undefined "material" (NFR6, FR3, FR2) — FR2 lets the owner upload "files and text" and a form. FR3 generates from "uploaded material and form answers". Does a filled form with no files count as material? NFR7 says "visual material", and FR10's deferral ("projects without visual material") suggests the MVP requires at least one visual. *Fix:* define "material" in §2.1 and state the minimum input that enables generation, for example "≥1 image or video deliverable".
- **low** NFR4's bound reads as a range (§4 NFR4) — "15-20 minutes at most" — the bound is 20. *Fix:* "at most 20 minutes (target 15)". Also say whether upload transfer time counts as hands-on time.

## Scope honesty — strong

§10 does real work. It names the "Crea case" button, the project-close trigger, in-tool posting, in-tool requests and in-project tagging, each one an omission a reader could otherwise assume. §11 is candid about the root cause, missing material, pre-auth client data and schedule density. Open-items density is moderate: 2 `[ASSUMPTION]` tags, 1 `[NOTE FOR PM]`, and 9 live open points. That is acceptable for an internal demo, but the open points are too many for Sprint 2 to start cleanly unless they get dated (see Decision-readiness).

### Findings
- **high** Guardrail NFR1 conflicts with the FRs and the conflict isn't flagged (§4 preamble, NFR1, FR4, FR7, addendum line 19) — §4 says contradicting requirements "must be flagged, not implemented". But NFR1 says the AI does not alter "graphic assets … or template design", while FR4 has templates adapt their layout. FR7 and open point 1 imply cropping or resizing the same assets into 16:9, 4:5, 1:1, 9:16 and A4. The configurator has "shuffle layout" and "replace media". None of this is flagged. A developer can't tell whether auto-cropping an image to 1080×1350 violates NFR1. *Fix:* add a `[NOTE FOR PM]` or a clarifying line to NFR1. Separate deterministic template operations (scale, crop to frame, reflow), which are allowed, from AI modification of asset pixels or template design, which is prohibited. Say whether AI may choose the crop.
- **medium** The NFR2 vs NFR4 tension isn't acknowledged (NFR2, NFR4, line 65) — A manual check of "every claim" across a 12-slide deck plus carousel text, with no source-linking support, has to fit inside 20 minutes. The counter-metric protects review quality, but the PRD doesn't admit the two NFRs can collide, or say which wins. *Fix:* state that NFR2 takes priority over NFR4. Optionally, add a non-goal or a backlog note for claim-to-source highlighting.
- **medium** Shareable links built before link access is decided (FR7, Epic 5 in Sprint 2, open point 11, §11) — Epic 5 builds hosted links of real client material in Sprint 2, before login exists and before open point 11 (public vs logged-in vs expiring) is settled. §11 covers pre-auth uploads but not pre-auth public hosting. *Fix:* make open point 11 a Sprint 2 gate. Or make links default to disabled or logged-in-only until it is resolved.

## Downstream usability — adequate

Numbering is stable and resolves: FR1–10, NFR1–7, Epics 2–8 with Epic 1 marked as retired, and open point 5 marked as merged. The §2.1 glossary helps. The epic prerequisites say what each epic needs. Gaps for architecture and stories: key nouns are missing from the glossary ("material", "guardrails", "template", status names), there is no FR for template maintenance, and the admin permission model is ambiguous.

### Findings
- **high** Release plan vs epic dependencies (§5, §6, §8, §11) — §5 says epics run "one after another … never in parallel". Sprint 2 (Oct 15–21, one week) has to deliver Epics 3, 4 and 5 in sequence. Epic 3's prerequisites (line 193) include "final case study templates" and "narrative structure and quality criteria", which are Sprint 2 non-dev deliverables. That contradicts §6's own rule that non-dev work is a prerequisite "for the epics in the same sprint or the next one", because the first epic in a sequential sprint can't start until those finish. The prerequisite "graphic template layouts" appears in no sprint. §11 names the density but not the ordering contradiction. *Fix:* move final templates and the narrative structure into Sprint 1 non-dev (or the start of Sprint 2 with a date). Add "graphic template layouts" to a sprint. Name a fallback cut for the demo, for example a single export format.
- **medium** Template maintenance has no FR or story (§2 role table, Story 6.2, NFR1) — The owner/designer "maintains templates" and has a "manage templates" permission, but no FR or story defines what maintaining means: upload, edit, versioning. NFR1 meanwhile forbids the AI from touching template design. Architecture can't size this. *Fix:* add an FR (or state that templates are seeded by the team and not editable in the MVP), and add it to §10 if it's out.
- **medium** The admin can edit permissions, which could bypass NFR7 (Story 6.2, NFR3, NFR7) — "As an admin, I want to manage roles and permissions" implies the admin can change what a role can do, for example grant account/sales/social the right to approve. That would defeat the owner-approval guardrail. NFR3 protects only "configuring the guardrails". *Fix:* state that role permissions are fixed in the MVP and the admin only assigns users to roles. Or state that approve and export permissions can't be reassigned.
- **medium** Guardrail access is undefined and untestable (Story 6.3, NFR3, §2 Admin row) — The table says "no access to guardrails", NFR3 says "configure", and Story 6.3 says "view or edit". NFR1, NFR2 and NFR6 are behaviours and a manual process, not a settings object, so it's unclear what a guardrail screen would be. *Fix:* say whether guardrails exist as configuration (for example prompt or system rules). If they do, keep "cannot view or edit". If not, rewrite Story 6.3 as "the tool exposes no setting that disables NFR1/NFR6/NFR7".
- **medium** The demo needs an export that roles may forbid (§1.1 line 45, FR7, Story 5.1, open point 9) — The demo success criterion requires the case study "exported in several formats". Under FR7 only account/sales/social users can export, and Epic 6 (Sprint 3) enforces roles before the Oct 30 demo. The PRD flags this as open point 9, but the demo plan (line 248) doesn't account for it. *Fix:* resolve open point 9 before Sprint 3, or script the demo with two logins (owner approves, sales exports). That would actually showcase NFR7.

## Shape fit — strong

An internal tool with three functional roles fits the capability-spec shape the PRD uses. FRs, guardrail NFRs, a sprint plan and epic stories are right, and the absence of UJs isn't a gap here. Keeping v1's structure and numbering was the right call for a team already working from v1. The addendum correctly holds UI detail out of the PRD and calls the A3 sketches "design sketches, not decisions". The success criteria and known risks added in v2 earn their place. The one shape strain is that story acceptance criteria carry all the done-ness, and they are the thinnest part (see Done-ness clarity).

## Mechanical notes

- **Assumptions Index missing.** Inline: `[ASSUMPTION]` FR2 upload limits (line 95), `[ASSUMPTION]` demo dry run (line 248), `[NOTE FOR PM]` counter-metric (line 65). No index. The addendum (line 16) states the 20 × 100 MB limit as fact, while the PRD marks it as an assumption. Align the two.
- **Status names.** Story 4.3 uses "Approved status" while open point 8 says the lifecycle is undefined. FR7 and the glossary use "publish" for links, and the addendum has a "Published" status. Pick one set of terms in the glossary when open point 8 closes.
- **Open point 1 epic tags.** These say "Epics 3 and 5", yet FR7 and Story 5.1 reference open point 1 and Epic 3 doesn't. That's consistent but worth making explicit in Story 3.1 (generation must target the chosen formats).
- **FR2 / NFR2 source scope.** FR3 allows "uploaded material and form answers", but NFR2 and Story 4.3 require claims to trace to "the uploaded material" only. Add "or form answers" so form-sourced claims don't count as violations.
- **FR6 pronoun.** "It belongs to the owner/designer who uploaded…" — "it" refers to the approval, not the case study. Reword.
- **Epic list numbering.** Item "1." is struck through but still numbered. That's fine and consistent with the stated convention.
- **Dates.** Sprint 3 is due Oct 28, the demo is Oct 30 and the MVP deadline is Oct 30. These are consistent. The guardrail check is a Sprint 3 non-dev activity after all generation epics are done, which leaves no rework time if it fails. Consider moving a first guardrail check to the end of Sprint 2.
- **Post-MVP stories 8.1–8.3** have no acceptance criteria. That's acceptable given there's no date.
